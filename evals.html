Testing & design

Tests shape the software

Testing is a selection pressure during the build.

  • An executable check makes intent concrete
  • Hard-to-test behavior pushes you toward smaller parts, clearer interfaces, and isolated dependencies
  • Run, change, repeat: feedback shapes the implementation while you’re building it

The evals we build today exert the same pressure.

The harness matters

Evaluate RAG retrieval

Semantic similarity is an engineering decision. Back it with data.

  • Embedding space
    Test paraphrases, domain terms, exact identifiers, and chunk boundaries against labeled relevance
  • Vector search
    Compare approximate search with exact neighbors. Check filters and missed matches
  • Keyword baseline
    Use the same queries and corpus. Compare relevance, p95 latency (including query embedding), and cost
MEASURE RETRIEVAL QUALITY
Precision@k
What fraction of the top k chunks is relevant?
Recall@k
What fraction of all relevant chunks appears in the top k?
F1
The harmonic mean of precision and recall at a chosen cutoff.

Use representative queries and labeled relevant chunks. Nearest in vector space does not guarantee relevance.

The hillclimbing loop

One change per round. Keep improvements; revert overfit or regression.

  • Choose one thing to edit, such as the prompt. Hold the model and the rest of the harness fixed.
  • Repeat baseline runs to understand normal score variation.
  • Read failures on the train split, form a hypothesis, and propose one change.
  • Score the change on train and held-out test. Keep it when both improve beyond the noise.
  • Revert regressions or gains that do not generalize. Repeat, keeping test examples out of the analyzer.
