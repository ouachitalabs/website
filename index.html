The opportunity

Operations are training infrastructure

The missing data is the work itself.

Today’s models can summarize a document or draft an email. They still struggle with work that crosses systems, rules, records, exceptions, and judgment calls.

The best examples of that work are not sitting in a public dataset. They live inside real companies—in the decisions experienced people make every day.

01 / Context

What the expert saw

Records, documents, policies, system state, history, and the exception in front of them.

02 / Judgment

What the expert noticed

The pattern, conflict, deadline, missing fact, or unwritten rule that changed the decision.

03 / Action

What they did next

The tools they used, the sequence they followed, and why they chose that path.

04 / Outcome

What actually happened

A measurable final state that can distinguish plausible work from successful work.

A pile of records is not the asset. The valuable signal is context + judgment + actions + outcome.

What we build

A flight simulator for AI workers

Practice the work without touching the live operation.

A pilot does not learn by immediately flying a passenger jet. An AI agent should not learn a consequential workflow by experimenting in a production account.

We recreate the important parts of the job in a controlled system. The agent gets a realistic starting situation, relevant files and data, and simulated tools. It can investigate, act, and reach a final state. Software checks and expert rubrics then determine whether the work succeeded.

In AI research, these are often called reinforcement-learning environments, agent environments, or evaluation environments.

Governance by design

The source system is not the training environment

Your production data does not become somebody else’s sandbox.

The goal is to preserve the logic and difficulty of the work while removing what a model builder does not need. A useful environment can often be built from de-identified, transformed, or synthetic representations instead of raw production access.

These are partnership design requirements, not claims that Ouachita Labs currently holds a particular certification or blanket compliance status. Controls, vendors, agreements, and permitted uses must be defined for each project before protected information moves.

  • Minimize accessStart with the smallest useful workflow and data surface.
  • De-identify firstRemove or transform patient, customer, and employee identity before approved external use.
  • Isolate productionUse controlled representations; never expose production credentials to a training system.
  • Approve what leavesDefine permitted packages, recipients, rights, retention, and uses in writing.
  • Keep an audit trailMake source, transformations, approvals, and releases traceable.
  • Handle PHI deliberatelyWhen PHI is involved, use HIPAA-appropriate infrastructure and agreements before access begins.
